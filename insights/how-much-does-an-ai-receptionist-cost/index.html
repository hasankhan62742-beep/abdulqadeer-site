How Much Does an AI Receptionist Cost?

For most small businesses, an AI receptionist costs $49 to $150 a month on a self-serve plan, according to a November 2026 pricing guide that read each vendor’s own pricing page. A human answering service costs far more for the same calls. The number that matters for your business is not the headline price, though — it is what you pay at your actual call volume, and that depends on how the plan counts your calls.

By Abdul Qadeer · 25 November 2026 · 7 min read

What an AI receptionist actually is

An AI receptionist is software that answers your business phone line in a natural-sounding voice, takes messages, answers common questions, and books appointments into your calendar — no person sitting at a desk required.

It picks up every call, day and night, and hands you a short summary of each one. What it does not do is think. If a caller is upset, confused, or asking something unusual, the software follows its script — it cannot read the room the way a person can. So the cheapest plan is not always the right one.

The three ways you’re charged

Every AI receptionist bills one of three ways, and the model decides your bill more than the headline price does.

Per minute. You buy a bundle of minutes each month. Rosie charges $49 a month for 250 minutes, and $149 for 1,000 minutes, according to the November 2026 pricing guide. Dialzara starts at $29 a month for 60 minutes, with extra minutes at $0.48 each. Short calls make this the cheapest route. Long, chatty callers make it the most expensive.

Per call. You pay for each call answered, however long it runs. Smith.ai’s AI receptionist is free for 25 calls a month, then $3.00 a call, and its Pro plan is $150 a month for 75 calls. If your callers talk for ten minutes, this beats per-minute pricing easily.

Per unique caller. You pay once per person, no matter how many times they ring that month. Goodcall charges $79 a month for 100 unique callers, $129 for 500, and $299 for 2,000. If the same customers call back two or three times a month, this is the model that saves you money.

What the bill looks like at your call volume

The same guide priced 100, 300 and 1,000 three-minute calls a month on the published plans. At 100 calls, the monthly bill ran from $79 for Goodcall to $1,725 for Ruby’s human receptionists. At 300 calls it ran from $129 to $2,100. At 1,000 calls, Goodcall came to $259 — while Smith.ai’s human receptionists reached $8,050.

The range is that wide because you are not comparing like with like. A $49 app answers and takes a message. A $300-a-month human service answers, qualifies the caller, and hands you a structured lead. The honest question is not which is cheapest — it is which job you are buying. Count the calls you get in a month first, because the cheapest plan for 80 calls is rarely the cheapest for 300.

AI versus a human receptionist

A human receptionist is still the most expensive way to answer the phone. Ruby’s published plans run $250 a month for 50 minutes, $395 for 100, $720 for 200, and $1,725 for 500 minutes. Smith.ai’s human receptionists start at $300 a month for 30 calls. On Ruby’s plans a human costs $3.45 to $5.00 a minute — between 17 and 34 times Rosie’s per-minute rate, according to the same November 2026 comparison.

Hiring someone yourself costs more still. The US Bureau of Labor Statistics puts the median receptionist wage at $18.27 an hour — about $38,010 a year (May 2025) — and that is wages alone, before taxes, benefits, sick days, and the hours nobody answers the phone. An AI receptionist does not replace a good receptionist. It replaces the calls that go unanswered — the ones at lunch, after hours, and while your front desk is already on the phone.

The costs that don’t show up in the headline price

Four charges catch owners out. First, overage: once you pass your bundle, extra minutes or calls are billed at a higher rate, and the invoice is where you find out. Second, add-ons: some plans charge extra per call for calendar booking, text confirmations, or syncing with your customer list. Third, spam: on a per-call plan, every spam call counts unless the vendor filters them first — Smith.ai, for example, filters known spammers before its AI answers. Fourth, setup: if the software cannot already reach your calendar or booking system, someone has to connect it, and that work goes on your bill.

Ask every vendor these three questions before you sign: what is the overage rate, what costs extra per call, and what happens to spam calls? If the answers are not in writing, the headline price is not the price.

Work out your number in ten minutes

Pull last month’s call record from your phone provider and write down three numbers: how many calls came in, how long they ran on average, and how many were from people who had already called that month. Those three numbers pick the pricing model for you — short calls favor per-minute, long calls favor per-call, repeat callers favor per-caller.

Then test before you buy. Smith.ai’s free AI plan covers 25 calls a month and Rosie offers a 7-day trial, so forward your after-hours calls to one of them for a week and count how many needed a booking. If the test week is quiet, a cheap plan is enough. If the phone never stops, you have just found the number your business actually needs — and the plan that fits it.
