What the Local Consumer Review Survey Says About Your Customers

The 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey — BrightLocal's annual survey of 1,002 US adults — found that 97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses, and 41% say they always read them before choosing one. If your reviews are old, few, or unanswered, customers are quietly moving on to the next name on the list.

By Abdul Qadeer · 24 November 2026 · 7 min read

Your customers check six places, not one

Before a customer calls you, they check around: the average consumer uses six different review sites when choosing a business, according to BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey. Google is still the biggest, but its share slipped from 83% in 2025 to 71%.

ChatGPT and other AI tools jumped from 6% to 45%, now the third most popular source of local recommendations. Apple Maps nearly doubled, from 14% to 27%. Your customers are not looking in one place any more.

Worse, only about 35% of small and medium businesses have a Google Business Profile at all, according to BrightLocal's own research. If yours is missing or half-finished, customers cannot find you or leave you a review.

Takeaway: search for your business on Google, Facebook and Apple Maps. Claim anything unclaimed, and make the hours, address and number match everywhere — one wrong number can lose a customer who was ready to call.

The bar keeps rising

Having reviews is not enough — customers expect a lot of them, recent ones, and a high rating: 47% will not use a business with fewer than 20 reviews, and only 9% will use one with five or fewer, according to BrightLocal's 2026 survey.

The rating they want has moved up too: in 2026, 31% will only use a business rated 4.5 stars or higher, up from 17% a year earlier, and 68% want four stars or more, up from 55%. And 74% only care about reviews from the last three months — a profile whose newest review is from last spring reads like a business that has gone quiet.

But the single most important factor is not the stars. When BrightLocal asked what makes a review trustworthy, the top answer — chosen by 56% — was that other reviews say the same thing. One glowing review does not carry you; a steady run of recent ones, all saying roughly the same good things, does.

Takeaway: twenty reviews is a sensible first target, then keep them coming — a handful of old five-star reviews is worth less than a fresh, consistent pile.

Good reviews don't close the sale — they start the research

After reading positive reviews, 66% of consumers do more research, and only 34% are ready to buy or book straight away, according to BrightLocal's 2026 survey.

The most common next step is your website: 54% visit it after reading positive reviews, up from 32% in 2019. Another 37% read more reviews, 24% check social media, and 20% contact the business.

That research is worth real money: 93% have bought something after reading reviews, and 27% have spent more than $1,000 on the back of them. But only about 40% of local businesses have a dedicated website at all, BrightLocal found — so plenty of customers who want to check you out hit a dead end.

Takeaway: your reviews send people to your website, so make sure it answers the obvious questions fast — what you do, what it costs, when you are open, how to reach you. Slower than a minute, and they drift to the next listing.

Your replies are part of the review

Customers read your replies as closely as the reviews: 80% are more likely to use a business that responds to every review, while 42% are unlikely to use one that ignores its reviews entirely, according to BrightLocal's 2026 survey.

And they do not want to wait: 89% expect the owner to respond, 19% the same day — up from 6% last year — and 81% within a week.

A copy-paste reply does more harm than good: half of consumers say a generic response makes them unlikely to choose the business.

Takeaway: reply to every review, in your own words, and do it fast. A short reply mentioning what the customer actually said beats a polished template every time.

AI now reads your reviews for the customer

There is a new reader in the room, and it is not a person: 82% of consumers read AI-generated summaries of reviews, and 23% are happy to rely on the summary alone, according to BrightLocal's 2026 survey. Meanwhile 40% trust AI platforms for recommendations, and 42% trust them as much as traditional reviews.

That matters because the summary is written from your reviews and your listings. Wrong hours on one site, an old address on another — that is what the summary says about you, and a customer asking ChatGPT may never see your business at all.

Takeaway: keep your information accurate everywhere. AI tools are quoting you now, whether you are watching or not.

What to fix this week

You do not need a reputation agency — you need an hour and a short list. First, search your business on Google, Facebook and Apple Maps and fix anything wrong. Second, count your reviews and check the date of the newest one; if it is older than a month, ask this week's happy customers. Our guide on how to get customers to leave reviews shows the simplest way to ask.

Third, reply to every review from the last month in your own words. Fourth, open your own website on your phone: can a stranger find your services, prices and contact details in under a minute? Fifth, put the ask on rails — a short text after every job, sent automatically with the review link inside, which is exactly what business automation is for.

Takeaway: reviews are not a chore for later. They are the first thing your next customer reads — and the reason they call you, or the next name on the list.
