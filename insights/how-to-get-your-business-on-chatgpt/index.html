How to Get Your Business on ChatGPT

You cannot buy a place in ChatGPT's answers, and there is no form to submit. To get your business recommended, claim your free profile in ChatGPT's Business Hub, make sure its search crawler can read your website, and keep your business details identical everywhere your customers — and AI — look. Your buyers are already asking ChatGPT who to hire — and it names the businesses it can find, understand and trust.

By Abdul Qadeer · 26 November 2026 · 7 min read

Claim your profile — it's new, and it's free

ChatGPT now lets businesses claim their own profile at chatgpt.com/business-hub — free, and a November 2026 walkthrough says to plan on 20 to 30 minutes, plus a short review. Search for your business first — ChatGPT has probably already built a listing for you, so claim that one.

Two checks. First, prove you control the business: ChatGPT sends a code to your business phone, or you add a small text record to your domain settings. Then the owner verifies identity with a photo ID and a live selfie — the person on the ID has to hold the phone, so your agency cannot do this step for you. In the walkthrough, review took about 30 minutes.

Read the terms before you click through. Claiming the profile does not hand you control of the story: OpenAI can still change what it shows about you using other sources, which is exactly why the rest of this article matters. Your basic details can stay on display even if you stop managing the profile. And be clear on what claiming does not do — OpenAI says it does not guarantee higher placement or more recommendations. What you get is correct facts on display, and data on how often ChatGPT brings you up. One more thing to know: ChatGPT may call your business on behalf of its users, so warn your team — there is an opt-out in the terms.

Let ChatGPT's crawler read your website

This work has a name — GEO, short for generative engine optimization — but for a small business it is mostly good local marketing.

ChatGPT's search uses a crawler called OAI-SearchBot to read websites. OpenAI says plainly that sites that opt out of it "will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers." So check your robots.txt file — the small file that tells crawlers what they may read — and ask whoever manages your website to confirm the crawler is allowed and nothing else is blocking it. OpenAI says a change takes about 24 hours to register. Do not confuse it with GPTBot, a different crawler that collects content for training — blocking that one changes nothing in search.

Say plainly what you do — one page per service

In a tracker that read 9,493 ChatGPT answers naming a vendor between 5 October and 3 November 2026, the named business's own page was cited in the same answer 82% of the time — and about 54% of those citations went to a service or product page, against 28% for the home page and 18% for articles.

Give each core service its own page, and open it with a plain sentence: what you do, for whom, and where. A plumber's page should say "emergency drain repair in Dallas for homeowners" before anything clever. Then add what a shortlist needs: how pricing works, typical customers, and how to start.

Put your proof in plain text. A star rating trapped inside a widget, a price inside a PDF, or results shown only in a photo slider cannot be read or repeated. Write it out: your rating and review count, your starting price, how long you have been licensed. Do not build a separate page for every question variation — Google warns that pages made mainly to influence its AI answers violate its scaled content abuse policy.

Make your details match everywhere

ChatGPT has to be sure the business on your website, your Google profile and a review site is the same business. Use the exact same name, address, phone, hours and website on your Google Business Profile, Yelp, Bing Places and your industry's directories. If two sources disagree, the AI has to guess.

Claim your Yelp page even if your customers rarely use Yelp. Yelp told investors in August 2026 that its ratings and reviews "recently began powering ChatGPT's local experience in relevant categories" — your Yelp page is now part of what ChatGPT reads about you.

Then keep earning reviews that mention specifics. Ask every customer the same way, right after the job, with a direct link. A review that says what you did and where beats ten that just say "great service" — our guide to getting customers to leave reviews walks through the habit step by step. Finally, get named on other people's pages — a local news story, your chamber of commerce, an industry list. In the same tracker, "best X" lists showed up in about a third of ChatGPT's answers that named a vendor.

Test it yourself, once a month

Write down ten questions the way a buyer would ask them — "best plumber in Austin," "who fixes water heaters near me" — and ask each one in a fresh ChatGPT chat on three different days. Note who gets named and which links appear.

Do it on three days because a single answer says very little. In the tracker, only 20.4% of the business-and-question pairs ChatGPT named were named on more than half of the days measured. One day's answer proves nothing; a month of notes is what counts. Retest the same questions four weeks later.

If a competitor shows up and you do not, work the steps above in order — claim, crawler, pages, listings, mentions — and test again. That is the whole system.

What won't work

Owners keep trying three shortcuts. Paying for placement — OpenAI says "ads do not influence ChatGPT's answers." Asking ChatGPT about your own business over and over — nothing in OpenAI's documentation says your chats change what other people are told. Adding an llms.txt file — Google says plainly that it ignores these files.
