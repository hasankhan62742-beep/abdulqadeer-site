Where AI projects usually stall

01Demos that never reach your team

An impressive demo in a meeting means nothing if no one in your business can use it. Real AI needs clean data, honest testing, and a system that runs every day — not a one-off experiment.

02Answers nobody trusts

If a system cannot show why it gave an answer, your team will quietly stop using it. Trust comes from proof — testing on unseen data and a plain-word reason for every answer.

03Hours spent checking by hand

People reading documents or inspecting photos one by one — slowly, and with mistakes. AI does the first pass; your people handle the judgment calls.

04Generic tools that do not fit

Off-the-shelf AI does not know your products, your customers or your documents. Custom AI is built on exactly the information your decisions depend on.

What I need from you to start

Custom AI starts with your records, so the first thing I need is access to the data behind the decision you want to improve — sales history, customer records, the documents your team checks, or the photos that need reviewing. The data does not need to be perfect; real business data rarely is. It does need to include the outcome: who left, what sold, what was approved. Without the outcome recorded, there is nothing to learn from.

I also need one person who understands how the decision is made today — usually the person who makes it. A short conversation about what a good answer looks like, and what a bad one costs, shapes the whole project. With the data and that conversation, I can tell you honestly whether AI will help, and what the first useful version would look like.

If you are not sure whether your data is enough, that is what the first step is for. We agree on the decision and how you will know it worked, and I look at what you actually have before anything is built. If the data cannot support the job, you will hear that early — not after weeks of work.

What happens after handoff

When the system goes live, you receive the working software, the test report showing how it performed on data it had never seen, and documentation explaining what it does, where it is weak, and what would improve it. Your team uses it the same way they would use any other tool — it arrives connected to your existing software, or as an application they open.

AI is not a build-and-forget purchase. As new information arrives, the system is kept up to date and watched so problems are caught early. When you want it to do more — a second decision, a new type of document, a new data source — that is scoped as a separate piece of work, in writing, with a fixed price. The system stays yours throughout: your data, your software, and documentation another developer could pick up.

And the honesty does not stop at handoff. If the system starts making mistakes, or the data changes in a way that weakens it, the limits are written down and the fix is discussed openly — the same way the weaknesses were written down before you trusted it.
