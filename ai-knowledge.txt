## FAQ (short answers, as on the About page)
- What data do I need? Historical records tied to the outcome: orders and inventory for demand, customer activity and status for churn. The first review checks whether coverage, labels and time range are useful, before any build is discussed.
- Can you guarantee a specific accuracy? No responsible builder promises accuracy before seeing data. He establishes a baseline, chooses metrics matching the business risk, validates on held-out data, and documents limitations.
- How long does a project take? Depends on data readiness, integrations and delivery form. A focused prototype comes first; a focused first project usually takes 2–4 weeks after the free strategy call.
- How is pricing decided? After data and delivery needs are understood, the smallest useful phase is scoped with clear deliverables. You receive the scope in writing before work begins; infrastructure costs stay visible. The site lists no prices.
- What do I receive at handoff? The agreed working system (API, dashboard or automation) plus testing notes, documentation, deployment guidance and known limitations.
- What does an AI/ML engineer do for a business? Turns business data into working systems: models that forecast demand or flag churn, automation that removes repetitive work, dashboards that support decisions. Deployed software tied to a business outcome, not research.
- How can ML predict customer churn? By learning from historical customer behavior (usage, support interactions, billing history), a classification model scores who is likely to leave so the business can act first. It needs labeled historical data and a clear definition of churn.
- What is predictive modeling, and when should a company use it? Using historical data to estimate future outcomes (demand, risk, churn). Use it when there is enough recorded history, the decision repeats often, and being right more often has real monetary value.
- When should a business choose n8n, Make, or Zapier? n8n suits technical teams wanting self-hosted control and complex logic; Make fits visual multi-step workflows at moderate scale; Zapier fits simple no-code integrations quickly. Depends on data volume, custom logic and who maintains it.
- How do you evaluate whether a model is reliable? Validating on held-out data it never saw, metrics matching the business risk (e.g. recall for churn), stability over time, and documented limitations.
- How much does custom AI development cost? It depends on data readiness and scope. After the first review you receive a written scope with fixed deliverables, so the price is agreed before work begins. The site lists no prices — never quote a figure.
- What can AI do for my small business? Forecast demand to stock correctly, flag customers likely to leave, automate repetitive admin with n8n or Make, and answer common customer questions with a chatbot. Start with one painful, repeated decision — not a grand platform.
